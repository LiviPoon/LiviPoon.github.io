---
title: '"But, I only give people two chances."'
tags:
  - introspection
  - poem
date: 2026-03-10
---
Just wait until you meet someone whose smile
feels like the first light after your long dark,
the kind that finds you after barren years
and lays a hand upon your tired heart.

Someone who, with the smallest touch, could break
the fragile parts of you you thought were safe,
and with the slightest distance, leave you raw—
and still, you’d pardon them each time they did.

Someone who pulls forgotten parts of you
up from the depths you never knew were there,
then teaches you to grow beyond your pain,
beyond the self you thought you had to be.

Someone who still believes in who you are
when all the world has fallen cold and mute,
who cheers for your survival even when
no one else sees the war you’ve had to fight.

Someone before whom you can come undone
without the weight of shame, without disguise,
and place your darkest secrets in their hands
without the fear they’ll turn them into knives.

Just wait until you meet someone your heart
has begged itself to leave behind for good,
again, again, again—yet still remains
in every corner of your living mind.

Someone whose memory does not dissolve,
but lingers softly, sharply, like a ghost—
relentless in its tenderness and ache,
too near to lose, too distant to outrun.

Someone who, with the barest thought of them,
brings back the butterflies, and with them grief,
as though your heart had never truly learned
the shape of life that did not include them.

And if you ever found your way back there,
you would not ask them to become the past.
You’d let the older version of them go,
and learn them as they are before you now.

With open hands and all your tender ache,
you’d learn each scar, each silence, every softness,
and love the person standing in that light,
instead of grieving who they used to be.

And even if, through countless other lives,
you never got the chance to see them twice,
you still would bless this single life for this:
you once were brave enough to forgive again.

Because although they may have only been
a passing mercy through your life once more,
it still would have been worth the cost of love
to know them once again, and let them in.

Just *wait*.
